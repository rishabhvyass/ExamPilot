# Build ExamPilot on Gemini, Firebase, and guardrails

ExamPilot's AI assistant should run on **Google's Gemini API, with a few dollars of billing turned on from day one**. Groq's free plan is the fallback and Cloudflare Workers AI the backup after that. Every call should go through a small server-side proxy that holds the keys and caps each student's daily use. "Free" exists, but it is the wrong default for this app. Gemini's free tier lets Google use prompts for training and lets human reviewers read them. Its terms tell developers not to send personal information. Google no longer publishes the free-tier limits, and the last public figure was a cut to about 20 requests per day. The paid Flash-Lite tier costs roughly a tenth of a US cent per chat turn or admit card, so a cheap paid tier is safer than a free one that conflicts with an app full of student documents. NotebookLM (renamed Gemini Notebook in July 2026) cannot be embedded or driven from the app. Its only API is an enterprise-only, preview-stage Cloud API with no chat endpoint. ExamPilot should build its own slim "chat with my notes" feature on Gemini's native PDF input and keep a plain "Open in NotebookLM" link as an optional extra. Admit cards should follow an extract-and-discard pipeline. Gemini reads the card into a strict schema that excludes name, photo and enrolment number. Code checks the dates against the printed weekday, the student confirms an editable table, and the file is deleted. That approach anticipates India's DPDP Rules, whose core duties start on 13 May 2027. For the rest of the stack, use Firebase Auth and Firestore on the free Spark plan, keep PDFs on the device at first, and use react-pdf, Recharts and Motion on the web. Four decisions remain: an age policy for under-18 users, when to start paying for Gemini, how to store PDFs in the cloud, and whether Supabase's convenience is worth its pausing and India-block risks.

## Only two AI providers are production-capable at zero cost

Most "free LLM API" lists don't hold up against current terms. **Cerebras now offers only a 30-day, $5 trial** ([Cerebras](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/support/rate-limits)). **Hugging Face gives free users no monthly credits** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-providers/pricing)). GitHub Models' free limits are "intended to help you get started with experimentation" ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/github-models/use-github-models/prototyping-with-ai-models)). Cohere trial keys are evaluation keys capped at **1,000 calls a month** ([Cohere](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/rate-limits)). Mistral's free Experiment plan needs a verified phone number, and **requests on it may be used to train Mistral's models** ([Mistral](https://help.mistral.ai/en/articles/455206-how-can-i-try-the-api-for-free-with-the-experiment-plan)). That leaves four real options.

| Provider | Free allowance (Oct 2026) | Structured JSON for plans/charts | PDF / long context | Data use on free tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini API (3.8 Flash, 3.5/3.1 Flash-Lite) | Unpublished; shown per project in AI Studio | Strict JSON Schema, Zod in JS SDK | Native PDF up to 1,000 pages / 50 MB; 1M-token input | Used to improve products; human review possible |
| Groq (gpt-oss-120b/20b, qwen3.8-27b) | ~30 RPM, 1K RPD, 8K TPM, 200K TPD per model (page ambiguous on plan) | Strict mode, but not with streaming or tools | No PDF input; 8K TPM blocks whole documents | No retention by default; Zero Data Retention available |
| Cloudflare Workers AI | 10,000 Neurons/day | JSON mode on some models (unverified) | Small context; embeddings (bge-m3) available | Not checked |
| OpenRouter `:free` models | 50 req/day, or 1,000/day after a one-time 10-credit purchase | Tool calling; structured outputs not advertised | Varies; free list rotates | Depends on provider; not guaranteed training-free |

Sources: [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), [Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits), [Gemini structured output](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output), [Gemini document processing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/document-processing), [Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits), [Groq structured outputs](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs), [Groq data](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data), [Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/), [OpenRouter limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits), [OpenRouter privacy](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/privacy/logging).

Gemini wins on capability. **Gemini 3.8 Flash takes 1,048,576 input tokens and accepts PDF, image, audio and video.** It supports structured outputs, function calling, context caching and file search ([model page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.8-flash)). One model can therefore chat, emit study-plan and chart JSON, answer questions over a student's PDFs and read admit-card photos. The catch is the terms. Free-tier data is marked **"Used to improve our products: Yes"**, and the Additional Terms say human reviewers may read inputs and outputs. They also say **"Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services"** ([Gemini terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). Capacity is the second problem. Google stopped publishing free limits after cutting free Gemini 2.5 Flash to **5 RPM / 20 RPD in December 2025** ([agentdeals.dev](https://agentdeals.dev/gemini-api-pricing-changes)). Quotas also apply **per project, not per key**, so every student shares one pool ([rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)).

The paid tier fixes both problems for very little money. The October 2026 paid rates per million tokens (input/output) are **$0.25/$1.50 for 3.1 Flash-Lite, $0.30/$2.50 for 3.5 Flash-Lite and $0.75/$3.75 for 3.8 Flash** ([pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). The page flags some increases from 1 December 2026 or 1 January 2027. By my arithmetic, a chat turn of about 1,000 input and 500 output tokens on 3.1 Flash-Lite costs about **$0.001**, and a 4,000-token study-plan generation on 3.8 Flash costs about **$0.009**. A thousand chat turns therefore cost roughly a dollar. Paid usage is not used for product improvement ([terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)), and monthly spend caps exist ([agentdeals.dev](https://agentdeals.dev/gemini-api-pricing-changes)). For a student developer, "near-free" Gemini is the practical choice.

Groq is the best free fallback. Its strict JSON mode works on gpt-oss and qwen3.8-27b. However, **"Streaming and tool use are not currently supported with Structured Outputs,"** and strict mode requires every field to be `required` with `additionalProperties: false` ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs)). That limit points to the right design for every provider. Chat should be a streaming free-text call. Plan and chart generation should be a separate non-streaming call that returns `{date, subject, topic, minutes}` rows plus a chart spec, checked with Zod on the server and retried once if validation fails. Generated plans should be cached in the database and regenerated only when the student asks, so most daily load is cheap chat. By my rough estimate, chaining Gemini, then Groq's two models, then Workers AI, then OpenRouter could serve **about 30–60 light users a day for free**. Gemini's free quota is unknown, which makes that estimate weak, and it explains why billing should be on before launch.

Keys must never ship in the React bundle. **Cloudflare Workers Free allows 100,000 requests a day**, and time spent waiting on the LLM does not count against its 10 ms CPU limit ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Supabase Edge Functions allow 500,000 invocations a month ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/pricing)). Firebase AI Logic is a managed Gemini proxy with App Check and per-user rate limits on by default ([Firebase AI Logic](https://firebase.google.com/docs/ai-logic)). Whether it works on the free Spark plan is unconfirmed. A Cloudflare Worker that verifies the Firebase ID token, enforces caps such as 20 chats and 3 plan regenerations per student per day, and fans out to Gemini, Groq or Workers AI is the most flexible free option.

## NotebookLM cannot live inside ExamPilot, so rebuild its core

On **16 July 2026 Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook**. The announcement mentioned integration with the Gemini app and no developer access ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/notebooklm-gemini-notebook/)). The consumer product has no public API. The only official API belongs to the Enterprise edition, a **v1alpha Preview** Discovery Engine API. It can create, share and delete notebooks and add sources, and it has **no chat or query method** ([notebooks API](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/api-notebooks)). Enterprise needs a subscription of **15 to 5,000 licences** and sign-in through Cloud Identity or an enterprise IdP. Notebooks can be shared only with users in the same Cloud project, never publicly ([licensing](https://docs.cloud.google.com/agentspace/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/set-up-licensing); [overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/agentspace/notebooklm-enterprise/docs)). It is built for a company's own staff, not for the public using a student app. The standalone Podcast API that turned text into audio overviews is deprecated, and **Google isn't allowlisting new customers** ([Podcast API](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/notebooklm-enterprise/docs/podcast-api)).

Unofficial libraries such as `notebooklm-py` call undocumented endpoints using browser cookies, and their own README describes them as suited to prototypes ([notebooklm-py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teng-lin/notebooklm-py/main/README.md)). Using them for students would mean collecting students' Google session credentials, which is unacceptable. The only legitimate integration is a link. Owners can publish a notebook as "Anyone with a link", and viewers with a Google account can chat with it, but no embedding or URL scheme for adding sources is documented ([NotebookLM Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16322204?hl=en)). One India-specific detail makes the in-app route more attractive. **NotebookLM always applies a visible watermark to generated outputs in India**, even on paid tiers ([NotebookLM Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16213268?hl=en)).

The replacement is simpler than it sounds. Gemini processes PDFs **up to 1,000 pages or 50 MB at 258 tokens per page**, so a 300-page textbook is about 77K tokens. On Gemini 3 models, text already embedded in the PDF is not billed ([document processing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/document-processing)). For the MVP, send the student's PDF with an instruction to answer only from the document and to cite `[p. N]`. Flashcards, quizzes and mind maps are just other JSON-schema generations over the same document. Two caveats apply. Files API uploads expire after **48 hours**, so ExamPilot must keep its own copy. Model-produced page numbers can also be wrong. Phase two should switch to retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). In that design, pdf.js extracts text per page so page numbers are exact, the text is split into chunks, the chunks are embedded with `gemini-embedding-2` or Cloudflare's bge-m3, and answers cite pages the reader can click ([embeddings](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/embeddings)). Self-hosted clones exist, among them **Open Notebook (MIT), AnythingLLM (MIT), kotaemon and SurfSense (Apache-2.0)**, but each needs an always-on Docker server, and Khoj's AGPL licence is risky for a hosted app ([Open Notebook](https://github.com/lfnovo/open-notebook); [AnythingLLM](https://github.com/Mintplex-Labs/anything-llm); [kotaemon](https://github.com/Cinnamon/kotaemon); [comparison](https://rohitraj.tech/en/notes/open-notebook-vs-khoj-vs-surfsense-notebooklm-2026)). For a solo developer, a serverless Gemini pipeline is less work. kotaemon's page-highlighted citations are a good design reference.

## Admit cards: read with Gemini, check in code, confirm, then delete

Hall-ticket layouts vary too much for a template parser. A real Mumbai University-affiliated card puts the course code and title in one cell ("CSC301 ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS - III"). It writes dates as **"02/06/2026, TUESDAY"** and times as **"02:30 PM to 05:30 PM"**, prints three tickets per page, and carries the student's name, seat number, university code, photo and signature ([SIES GST sample](https://siesgst.edu.in/docs/exam/AIML%20SEM%20III%20R-19%20KT.pdf)). Other cards will use DD-MM-YY, FN/AN session codes, 24-hour times or practical exams with a date range. Accuracy work should therefore check the model's output rather than trust it.

The three extraction options compare as follows. **Gemini Flash-Lite with a JSON schema** handles text PDFs, scanned PDFs and phone photos in one call. Its output is schema-valid, but Google warns that schema-valid output can still be semantically wrong and that apps must validate values ([structured output](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output)). **pdf.js `getTextContent()`** gives exact positioned text from text-based PDFs, free and on the device, but it returns nothing for scans ([DEV](https://dev.to/bonzai2carn/the-three-tier-pdf-extraction-model-demystifying-pdfjs-52bc)). **Tesseract.js** runs in the browser but cannot read PDFs and uses plain Tesseract accuracy ([Tesseract.js](https://github.com/naptha/tesseract.js)). Cloud OCR, the third option, adds a vendor and still returns only raw text. Cloud Vision gives 1,000 free units a month, then $1.50 per 1,000 ([Vision pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vision/pricing)). Document AI has no free tier ([Document AI pricing](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/pricing)). Azure's free tier analyses only the first 2 pages per request at 1 transaction per second ([Azure limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/service-limits)). Gemini does OCR and structuring together for less than Vision charges for OCR alone, at about **₹0.08–0.1 per card** on paid Flash-Lite by my estimate.

The pipeline works like this. On the client, pdf.js runs first. If it finds real text with date-like tokens, the client strips lines labelled with name, seat or enrolment and sends only the text. Otherwise the page (or the photo, after fixing orientation and downscaling to about 2,000 px) goes to the vision path, ideally after the student crops out the candidate block. The server function calls paid Gemini Flash-Lite at temperature 0. The schema asks only for `subject_code`, `subject_name`, `date_raw`, `date_iso`, `start_time`, `end_time`, `session_label` and a per-row `confidence`, and never for name, enrolment number or photo. If validation fails, the server retries once on 3.8 Flash. Code then normalises dates as DD/MM with an explicit `dd/MM/yyyy` parse, never `new Date()`. It checks the weekday against the date, requires end times after start times and durations of 1–4 hours, flags clashes and inferred years, and removes duplicate rows from multi-ticket pages. The student sees an editable table with the raw string under each field, amber flags for "please check" and red flags that block saving. Only confirmed rows reach the calendar, followed by an .ics or Google Calendar export and a default "delete card now". The developer has no published accuracy benchmark for Indian hall tickets, so the first engineering task is a test set of **20–50 redacted real cards** across GTU, Parul, Mumbai University and other universities, measuring field-level edit rates.

Privacy drives the design. MeitY notified the **DPDP Rules on 13 November 2025**. The notice, security, breach, erasure and children's-data rules take effect on **13 May 2027** ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/11/14/meity-notified-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/)). Rule 3 requires a "clear, independent, and accessible" notice. Rule 6 requires encryption, access control and logging. Breaches must be reported to the Board within 72 hours. Rule 10 requires **verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18**. Maximum penalties reach **₹250 crore for security failures and ₹200 crore for children's-data breaches** ([PRS](https://www.prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2023/Summary_Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Bill_2023.pdf)). The cleanest compliance position is to process the file in memory, never persist it, store only the confirmed exam rows, and keep a minimal "card processed and deleted" audit log. Sending admit cards to Gemini's free tier conflicts directly with Google's warning against personal information, so this feature must use the paid tier, with Google named as a processor in the privacy notice. One point is still uncertain: the Seventh Schedule's one-year log-retention rule. Its application to audit logs, as opposed to the cards themselves, is my reading, not a verified legal one.

## A zero-cost stack: Firebase for identity and data, no cloud storage at first

| Layer | MVP pick (web) | Expo equivalent later | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auth | Firebase Auth (email/password + Google) | `@react-native-google-signin` + Firebase in a dev build | 50K MAU free on Spark; first-party Google sign-in |
| Database | Firestore (Spark) | Same | 1 GiB, 50K reads / 20K writes per day, no inactivity pause |
| File storage | Device only (IndexedDB) for study PDFs; admit cards never stored | Device file system | Firebase Storage needs Blaze |
| AI proxy | Cloudflare Worker (verifies Firebase token, per-user caps) | Same | 100K requests/day free |
| AI models | Gemini 3.1/3.5 Flash-Lite + 3.8 Flash (paid, capped); Groq fallback | Same | See above |
| PDF reader | react-pdf (+ react-pdf-highlighter-extended for highlights) | react-native-pdf or react-native-pdf-renderer | MIT, pdf.js-based, thumbnails |
| Charts | Recharts | Victory Native XL | Takes AI JSON arrays directly |
| Animation | Motion (`motion/react`, `layoutId` tabs); canvas-confetti; dotLottie | Reanimated 4, lottie-react-native, expo-splash-screen | Calm, reduced-motion aware |

Firebase Spark gives **50K monthly active users** for auth and Firestore with **1 GiB stored, 50K reads and 20K writes a day**, and its pricing page lists no inactivity pausing ([Firebase pricing](https://firebase.google.com/pricing)). Supabase's free plan is tempting because one plan covers a **500 MB Postgres database, 1 GB storage, Edge Functions and pgvector** ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). It has three problems, each of which would hurt a calm student app. Free projects **pause after a week of inactivity**, which is exactly what happens during exam breaks. The built-in email sender allows **2 emails per hour per project** until you configure custom SMTP ([Supabase rate limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/rate-limits)). And on **24 February 2026 Indian ISPs including Jio and Airtel blocked supabase.co** under a Section 69A order ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/india-disrupts-access-to-popular-developer-platform-supabase-with-blocking-order/)). Access came back on 3 March ([Lovable status](https://status.lovable.dev/incidents/01KJDBPNKJRQZPEDCY6MW95CYX)). Without a paid custom domain, Supabase's Google consent screen also shows `<project-id>.supabase.co` ([Supabase Google docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/social-login/auth-google)). Firebase's own weakness is storage: **Cloud Storage for Firebase now requires the Blaze plan**, and Spark projects get 402/403 errors. New buckets get the "Always Free" allowance only in three US regions ([Firebase Storage FAQ](https://firebase.google.com/docs/storage/faqs-storage-changes-announced-sept-2024)). The MVP sidesteps this by keeping study PDFs in IndexedDB on the device and syncing only metadata, highlights and progress. Clerk (branding stays on the free tier, 3 social connections) and Appwrite (pauses after 7 days without Console activity, deletes after 90 days paused) are weaker choices ([Clerk](https://clerk.com/pricing); [AgentDeals Appwrite](https://agentdeals.dev/vendor/appwrite)).

On the front end, **react-pdf** (MIT) provides `<Document>`, `<Page>` with text and annotation layers, and `<Thumbnail>` ([react-pdf](https://github.com/wojtekmaj/react-pdf)). Pages should be virtualised so that large textbooks render lazily. **Recharts 3.1.2** (MIT, about 8.8M weekly downloads) takes arrays of objects keyed by field names, so a validated AI spec like `{type, title, xKey, series, data}` renders without reshaping ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/recharts)). Victory Native XL can read the same spec later on Expo ([victory-native-xl](https://github.com/FormidableLabs/victory-native-xl)). **Motion's `layoutId`** gives the sliding tab indicator in about three lines ([Motion](https://motion.dev/docs/react-layout-animations)). **canvas-confetti's `disableForReducedMotion`** keeps celebrations accessible ([canvas-confetti](https://github.com/catdad/canvas-confetti)). On mobile, Reanimated 4.8 requires the New Architecture, and Moti still describes itself as "powered by Reanimated 3", so avoid Moti until it is confirmed to work with Reanimated 4 ([Reanimated](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/guides/compatibility); [Moti](https://github.com/nandorojo/moti)). The Google button must follow Google's branding rules, and the Google Identity Services button meets them automatically ([branding guidelines](https://developers.google.com/identity/branding-guidelines)).

## Conclusion

The search for a "free AI API" turns out to be mostly a question of terms, not money. The free tiers that are strong enough to matter trade away privacy, age eligibility or predictable capacity. The paid tier of the same Gemini model costs so little per student that the cheapest *safe* architecture is a spend-capped paid key behind your own proxy, with free providers as overflow, not foundation. That changes what the developer should optimise. The scarce resources are per-user caps, cached plans and good validation, not API credits.

The bigger strategic point is that ExamPilot should not try to out-feature NotebookLM, which already gives Indian students grounded chat, flashcards and audio for free. Its advantage is structure NotebookLM lacks: the exam calendar built from a hall ticket, plans tied to real dates, and progress charts. The admit-card pipeline is both the feature most likely to delight students and the one most exposed under DPDP. Building it as extract-and-discard now, before the May 2027 obligations take effect, turns a compliance burden into a trust message students can read on the upload screen.
